# Problem Statement

## Who is affected?
Busy urban households, students, and primary home cooks managing daily meal planning.

## What exactly is going wrong?
The edible leftovers that have already been cooked (like white rice, meat, and unused vegetables) are constantly being discarded since there is no culinary inspiration or knowledge to prepare new dishes from them before they expire. The conventional recipe websites find recipes based on the name of the dish but not based on leftover ingredients.

## Why does it matter?
This creates inefficiencies that directly raise expenses for consumers purchasing their food from supermarkets and generate unnecessary organic waste that finds its way to the city landfills, thus contributing to environmental degradation and carbon emissions. In Malaysia, residential households are the biggest generators of food waste, throwing away about 17,000 metric tons each day, with 24% of it being left-over edible food (The Star, 2024).

## Problem statement (3 to 5 sentences)
Busy households, students, and home cooks who must deal with daily meal planning routinely throw away edible, pre-cooked leftovers as they do not know how to immediately transform them into recipes that match the exact ingredient decay windows. This inefficiency because excessive household organic waste directly increases family living costs and significantly contributes to landfill accumulation and preventable greenhouse gas emissions. Households are the biggest contributor to food waste in Malaysia, with an estimated 17,000 tons of food wasted daily, according to the Solid Waste and Public Cleansing Management Corporation (SWCorp). Of this, more than 24% is edible leftover food that can be consumed or repurposed.
